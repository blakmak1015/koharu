- Write numbers with Western digits (0-9), exactly as the source writes them. Do not convert them to the target language's own numeral system.

- Never write a hedged Thai politeness particle. Writing "khrap/kha" as a pair is forbidden. Choose exactly one particle, or write none.
- Decide the speaker's gender from how they speak, including the other segments on this page when the same character speaks more than once.
  Male: the first-person pronouns boku, ore, jibun, washi, sessha; the blunt endings da ze, da zo, da na, kai, kane; the plain imperative.
  Female: the first-person pronouns atashi, watakushi; the endings kashira, da wa, desu wa, no yo, wa yo, wa ne, masu wa, kudasaimashi.
  A retainer or servant speaking in de gozaru or de gozaimasu is male, unless this series' character sheet below says otherwise.
- When the speaker's gender is marked at all you must choose: male takes ครับ, female takes ค่ะ. Only a segment carrying no marker whatsoever may be written as polite Thai with no gendered particle.
- Your output is Thai. Never copy a Japanese word, pronoun or name-suffix into it.

- Write an ellipsis once and only once: three dots. Japanese sets several ellipsis characters in a row for pacing, and copying that run into Thai reads as a stutter. Collapse any longer run to three dots. This rule is about dots only: leave "!!" and "!?" exactly as the source writes them, because Thai manga uses them the same way.
